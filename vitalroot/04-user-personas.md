# 04 — User Personas

## Persona Development Notes

These personas were built from overlapping research patterns in diabetes, obesity, CKD, heart disease, and behavioral health literature, and from health equity data on barriers to care. Each persona is composite, not stereotyped. Cultural details are included because they directly shape feature requirements — not as decoration.

---

## Persona 1: Marlene, 54

**"I've been trying to manage this for years. I just need something that actually gets me."**

### Demographics
- Age: 54 | Location: Birmingham, AL | Occupation: School cafeteria worker
- Household: Lives with husband + teenage granddaughter
- Income: ~$38,000/year combined
- Tech comfort: Moderate (uses Facebook, YouTube; texts regularly)

### Health Context
- Diagnosed: Type 2 Diabetes (7 years), High Blood Pressure
- A1C: 8.4 (above target)
- Takes Metformin + BP medication
- Has seen a diabetes educator once; doesn't have consistent dietitian access

### Food & Lifestyle
- Cooks most meals at home — Southern and soul food traditions
- Feeds a family of 3 on a careful budget (~$350/month grocery)
- Loves cooking greens, cornbread, beans, sweet potato
- Frustrated that "healthy eating" advice always tells her to throw away the food she loves

### Motivation Style
- Responds to: encouragement, seeing progress in small steps, feeling seen culturally
- Turned off by: calorie counting obsession, shame about weight, generic advice
- Likely to disengage if: she feels judged or if suggestions feel impossible given her budget

### Goals
- Lower A1C to a safer range
- Find recipes that feel like home but support her diabetes
- Understand which herbs (she takes fenugreek) are safe with her medications
- Find a local dietitian who takes her insurance

### Frustrations
- Most apps assume she'll eat grilled chicken and salad every day
- She can't afford to buy "health food store" ingredients
- She worries about the fenugreek she takes — no one has told her whether it's safe
- Feels invisible in wellness media

### Platform Use Patterns
- Morning mood check-in while coffee brews
- Browses recipes on Sunday for the week
- Uses chatbot to ask questions she's embarrassed to ask her doctor
- Would share recipes with her granddaughter

### Key Features for Marlene
- Soul food and Southern recipe library with diabetes adaptations
- Herb-medication interaction lookup (especially fenugreek + Metformin)
- Budget meal filter ($3 or less per serving)
- Gentle, non-shaming chatbot tone
- Local dietitian finder with insurance filter

## Persona 2: David, 41

**"I know I need to make changes. I just need someone to hold me accountable — not coddle me."**

### Demographics
- Age: 41 | Location: Houston, TX | Occupation: Logistics coordinator (desk job, long hours)
- Household: Wife + two kids (8, 11)
- Income: ~$72,000/year
- Tech comfort: High (uses apps for everything, smart watch)

### Health Context
- Diagnosed: Obesity (BMI 38), pre-hypertension
- Recently told by doctor to lose weight or expect Type 2 Diabetes diagnosis within 2 years
- No current medications, but concerned
- Has done multiple diets: keto (6 months), intermittent fasting (3 months), calorie counting (repeatedly)

### Food & Lifestyle
- Caribbean roots (Trinidadian family background)
- Loves doubles, roti, stew chicken, macaroni pie — family recipes are emotional touchstones
- Eats out 4x/week due to work schedule
- Exercises occasionally but no consistent routine; back pain limits some activities

### Motivation Style
- Responds to: direct feedback, data and numbers, visible progress metrics, mild challenge
- Turned off by: vague encouragement without action, being treated as fragile
- Likely to disengage if: app feels too soft or if he can't see clear progress

### Goals
- Build a consistent exercise habit (start with 3x/week)
- Reduce restaurant eating; find Caribbean recipes he can make quickly on weekdays
- Understand his risk numbers and what they mean
- Lose weight in a sustainable way without giving up his culture's food

### Frustrations
- Every healthy eating app ignores Caribbean food entirely
- He relapses after strict diets because they're not culturally sustainable
- Back pain makes many exercises impossible; apps never address this
- Wants accountability but not shame

### Platform Use Patterns
- Evening app check-in after kids are in bed
- Uses accountability-mode chatbot; wants blunt but kind feedback
- Tracks progress weekly, not daily (finds daily too anxiety-inducing)
- Would use grocery list feature if it saved him time

### Key Features for David
- Caribbean recipe library (Trinidad, Jamaica, etc.) with heart-health + weight adaptations
- Accountability-mode chatbot with data-focused check-ins
- Low-back-pain-aware exercise modifications
- Restaurant eating guidance (how to make better choices eating out)
- Progress dashboard with numbers he can see improving

## Persona 3: Rosa, 67

**"My kidneys are failing and nobody told me what I can and can't eat. I'm scared."**

### Demographics
- Age: 67 | Location: East Los Angeles, CA | Occupation: Retired (former seamstress)
- Household: Lives with adult son and his family; Spanish is primary language
- Income: Social Security + small savings
- Tech comfort: Low (uses phone for calls and WhatsApp; her son helps with apps)

### Health Context
- Diagnosed: Chronic Kidney Disease (Stage 3b), Type 2 Diabetes (12 years)
- On multiple medications; complex interaction concerns
- Sees a nephrologist every 3 months; no dietitian access
- Was told to limit potassium, phosphorus, sodium — has no idea what this means in practice for Mexican food

### Food & Lifestyle
- Mexican and Central American home cooking — rice and beans are a daily staple
- Doesn't understand why beans (which she thought were healthy) might need to be limited
- Her daughter-in-law cooks; Rosa wants to share safe guidance without feeling like a burden
- Takes herbal teas her family recommends — worried these may be dangerous

### Motivation Style
- Responds to: warmth, respect for her traditions, being taught (not told), faith references OK
- Turned off by: medical jargon, being made to feel stupid, feeling like her culture is wrong
- Likely to disengage if: she can't understand the language or the app assumes she can cook freely

### Goals
- Understand which traditional Mexican foods are safe for CKD
- Find safe alternatives to high-potassium/phosphorus staples
- Know whether the herbal teas she takes are dangerous with her medications
- Help her daughter-in-law cook kidney-safe meals without abandoning their culture

### Frustrations
- No Spanish-language support from her medical team
- Her cultural food is never acknowledged in health advice
- She's been told "avoid beans and potatoes" with no alternatives given
- Fears she's harming herself with traditional remedies

### Platform Use Patterns
- Would primarily use app with her son or daughter-in-law's help
- Needs Spanish-language support (Phase 2 priority)
- Would use herb safety lookup frequently
- Values printable/shareable content for family members

### Key Features for Rosa
- Kidney-safe recipes with Latin/Mexican cuisine focus
- High-potassium/phosphorus food list with cultural alternatives
- Herb safety database with Spanish content (Phase 2)
- Extra-prominent kidney + herb safety warnings
- Printable condition guides for family sharing
- Caregiver-accessible profile (Phase 2)

## Persona 4: James, 33

**"I had a stroke last year. I'm rebuilding from scratch and nobody tells me anything beyond 'take your meds.'"**

### Demographics
- Age: 33 | Location: Atlanta, GA | Occupation: Graphic designer (remote, part-time recovery)
- Household: Lives alone; parents and friends provide support
- Income: Variable; disability benefits + part-time freelance
- Tech comfort: High (used to use apps extensively; cognitive fatigue now limits this)

### Health Context
- Had an ischemic stroke 14 months ago (related to undetected hypertension)
- Currently in recovery: some word-finding difficulty, fatigue, mild left-side weakness
- Conditions now: hypertension, post-stroke cardiovascular risk
- On blood thinners (warfarin) — significant herb/food interaction concerns (vitamin K)
- Mental health: dealing with depression and grief related to identity loss

### Food & Lifestyle
- Ate mostly takeout before stroke; now needs to cook heart-healthy
- African American background; some cultural food connection but not a strong cook
- Vitamin K interactions mean even some vegetables need to be consistent (not avoided, but consistent)
- Fatigue makes complex cooking impossible on many days

### Motivation Style
- Responds to: acknowledgment of how hard this is, celebration of tiny wins, low-pressure guidance
- Turned off by: unrealistic expectations, toxic positivity, pressure to "bounce back fast"
- Likely to disengage if: he feels overwhelmed, the app is cognitively demanding, or he feels rushed

### Goals
- Understand heart-healthy eating within his vitamin K constraints
- Find simple, low-effort meals for low-energy days
- Rebuild motivation after depression and setback
- Understand what's safe vs. risky in natural remedies with warfarin

### Frustrations
- No one explained vitamin K management in plain language
- Depression after stroke is barely addressed in his care
- Most health apps assume users have normal energy and cognition
- Feeling like he should be "further along" in recovery

### Platform Use Patterns
- Short sessions (5–10 minutes maximum before fatigue)
- Relies heavily on chatbot for Q&A (avoids reading long articles)
- "I'm struggling today" flow will be critical
- Would benefit from push notifications with gentle, low-pressure check-ins

### Key Features for James
- Brain/neurological support page (education, hope, practical)
- Warfarin + vitamin K food interaction guidance
- Ultra-simple recipe cards for low-energy days (5 ingredients, 15 min)
- Cognitive-accessible app design (minimal reading, clear icons)
- "I'm struggling" flow with depression-aware escalation
- Celebration mode for every small win

## Persona 5: Tanya, 29

**"I've been trying to lose weight since I was 14. I don't need another app that makes me feel broken."**

### Demographics
- Age: 29 | Location: Chicago, IL | Occupation: Nursing student
- Household: Lives with roommate
- Income: ~$28,000/year (student + part-time work)
- Tech comfort: High (heavy app user, skeptical of wellness apps from past bad experiences)

### Health Context
- Diagnosed: PCOS (polycystic ovary syndrome), obesity
- No diabetes diagnosis yet; insulin resistance
- History of disordered eating (recovered, in therapy)
- Very cautious about tracking tools that trigger obsessive behaviors

### Food & Lifestyle
- Knows a lot about nutrition (nursing background) but struggles to apply it consistently
- Emotional eating patterns; food as comfort during stress
- Loves cooking; interested in plant-forward meals but not extreme restriction
- Budget-conscious student

### Motivation Style
- Responds to: autonomy, no tracking mandates, self-compassion framing, science-backed info
- Turned off by: calorie counts, "cheat day" framing, before/after photos, weight-focused messaging
- Likely to disengage if: app feels like another diet culture tool; if she sees anything that triggers restriction mentality

### Goals
- Manage insulin resistance through sustainable food choices (not restriction)
- Build a positive relationship with food and movement
- Find emotional support when stress eating happens without shame
- Learn which supplements (inositol, berberine) are evidence-based for PCOS

### Frustrations
- Most apps are built on diet culture and make her feel worse
- "Healthy" content often doesn't acknowledge PCOS specifically
- She's recovered from disordered eating but apps constantly put her at risk
- Wants evidence, not bro-science

### Platform Use Patterns
- Would turn off calorie tracking entirely; uses food log for pattern awareness only
- Chatbot is most valuable feature for her — judgment-free space to process
- Would blog/learn center articles on PCOS and insulin resistance
- Shares useful recipes with her nursing school peers

### Key Features for Tanya
- No mandatory calorie display (optional toggle)
- Recovery mode / gentle mode chatbot as default
- Non-weight-focused progress metrics (energy, mood, consistency)
- PCOS-specific content and recipes
- Supplement evidence database (inositol, berberine, etc.)
- Body-neutral language throughout the entire platform

## Persona 6: Marcus, 58

**"My wife has diabetes and heart disease. I do all the cooking. I need to help her without losing my mind."**

### Demographics
- Age: 58 | Location: Memphis, TN | Occupation: Retired military, now part-time security
- Household: Wife (57, Type 2 Diabetes + CHF) + adult son nearby
- Income: ~$55,000/year (pension + part-time)
- Tech comfort: Moderate (uses smartphone well; prefers simple interfaces)

### Health Context (as caregiver)
- Wife has Type 2 Diabetes and Congestive Heart Failure
- Managing fluid restriction, sodium restriction, diabetes-friendly diet simultaneously
- Caregiver stress: reports high stress, disrupted sleep, feeling isolated
- His own health: elevated cholesterol (could use the app for himself too)

### Food & Lifestyle
- Does all the cooking; uses Southern and soul food traditions
- Needs recipes that satisfy BOTH his needs and his wife's complex dietary restrictions
- Frustrated that recipes are either "too hospital bland" or not restricted enough
- Would love meal planning help for the week

### Motivation Style
- Responds to: practical tools, clear instructions, feeling capable and in control
- Turned off by: overly emotional or therapy-heavy content; he wants information and tools
- Likely to disengage if: the app only speaks to the patient, not the caregiver

### Goals
- Find recipes that meet both CHF and diabetes restrictions simultaneously
- Understand sodium and fluid restrictions in practical cooking terms
- Get help planning a week of meals without extensive research time
- Find caregiver support resources and community

### Frustrations
- No app or resource speaks to him as a caregiver, not the patient
- Cooking for complex multi-condition restrictions is overwhelming
- He neglects his own health while caring for his wife
- Feels isolated — no one understands what caregiving at this intensity is like

### Platform Use Patterns
- Uses app in evenings to plan next day's meals
- Would use meal planning feature extensively if it existed
- Interested in caregiver community/forum (Phase 3)
- Might use chatbot to ask practical cooking questions

### Key Features for Marcus
- Caregiver mode (Phase 2 roadmap → critical for this persona)
- Multi-condition recipe filtering (diabetes + heart + sodium simultaneously)
- Meal planning calendar with shopping list
- Soul food and Southern cuisine adaptations
- Caregiver support resources and emotional wellness content
- His own health profile (dual-user household mode)
