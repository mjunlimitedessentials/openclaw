# 01 — Product Strategy

## Vision Statement

VitalRoot Health is a compassionate digital companion that meets people where they are — not where wellness culture says they should be. It helps individuals with chronic conditions build sustainable, culturally resonant habits through personalized guidance, emotional intelligence, and trustworthy health education.

## Mission

To make personalized, condition-aware health support accessible to everyone, regardless of background, budget, cooking skill, or mobility — delivered with dignity and empathy, never shame.

## The Problem We Solve

| Problem | Current Reality | VitalRoot Approach |
|---------|----------------|-------------------|
| Generic wellness advice | "Eat salad and walk 10k steps" | Condition-specific, cuisine-aware, mobility-adaptive guidance |
| Shame-based health culture | Apps that penalize missed goals | Compassion-first, recovery-aware, no streaks that shame |
| Cultural erasure | Advice built around white Western diets | Soul food, Caribbean, African, Latin, Asian, Southern cuisine support |
| Information overload | 50 tabs open, contradicting each other | Curated, medically reviewed, plainly written content |
| Expensive professional access | Dietitians cost $150/hr | Educational content + local resource navigation at no/low cost |
| Medical system gaps | 7-minute appointments | Ongoing companion support between appointments |
| Crisis without a net | No escalation from wellness apps | Explicit escalation paths to crisis resources, clinician referral |

## Strategic Positioning

**Category:** Empathetic chronic condition wellness companion
**Not:** A medical app, telehealth platform, or calorie-counting tool
**Differentiation from competitors:**

| Competitor Type | What They Do | What We Do Differently |
|----------------|-------------|----------------------|
| MyFitnessPal | Calorie/macro tracking | No weight obsession; condition-aware, culturally inclusive food |
| Noom | Behavioral psychology coaching | More cultural representation; no shame mechanics |
| Virta Health | Reverse diabetes (keto-heavy) | Diet-agnostic; culturally aware; not keto-exclusive |
| Generic health blogs | Static educational content | Personalized delivery; interactive; app-linked |

## Core Product Principles

### 1. Compassion Before Compliance
Every feature decision must ask: "Does this shame or support?" If there's a chance it shames, redesign it. Progress bars show movement toward goals — never punish regression. Missing a week of check-ins triggers a gentle welcome back, never a streak counter set to zero.

### 2. Personalization Over Generic Advice
The app knows: condition(s), cuisine preferences, cooking skill, budget, mobility level, motivation style, and emotional state. Recommendations that ignore these variables are not shipped.

### 3. Cultural Humility as a Product Feature
Recipe libraries, example meal plans, and educational content must represent the full diversity of the user base. Specifically: soul food, Caribbean, African, Latin, Southern, Asian, Mediterranean, and plant-forward cuisines get first-class support — not an afterthought "cultural foods" section.

### 4. Safety as a Non-Negotiable
- No herb or supplement is presented as universally safe.
- Kidney disease users get special additional caution on herbs, supplements, and high-potassium/phosphorus foods.
- Crisis detection triggers escalation flows with professional resource links.
- All condition-specific advice carries medical disclaimer + reviewed-by field.

### 5. Trustworthy Content Architecture
Content hierarchy:
- **Reviewed** (clinician or registered dietitian reviewed) → badge displayed
- **Educational** (evidence-informed editorial) → clearly labeled
- **Community** (user contributions) → clearly labeled, moderated

### 6. Accessibility First
- WCAG 2.2 AA minimum target
- Screen-reader-friendly components
- High-contrast mode
- Font-size adjustable
- Motion-reduced mode
- Plain language (≤8th grade reading level target for core content)

## Regulatory + Legal Posture

- Platform is wellness/educational, not a medical device.
- No PHI storage that triggers HIPAA without explicit BAA — in Phase 3 B2B, pursue BAA.
- Terms of service clearly state: not a medical provider, not a substitute for professional care.
- All content carries "This is not medical advice" at appropriate levels.
- Clinician review program: at launch, secure at least 2 registered dietitians and 1 MD to review condition pages.
