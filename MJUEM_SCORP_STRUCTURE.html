## Two Ways to Structure the Brands

Recommended to start
Option A — DBAs under one S-Corp
  • One legal entity: MJUEM Co. (S-Corp)
  • Each brand is a registered DBA / trade name
  • One bank account, one tax return
  • Lowest cost, simplest bookkeeping
Trade-off: all brands share the same liability shield — a lawsuit against one reaches everything.
Move to this later
Option B — Subsidiary LLCs owned by the S-Corp
  • MJUEM Co. (S-Corp) owns single-member LLCs
  • Each brand = its own LLC, e.g., ThinkBox AI Operation Systems LLC
  • Taxes still roll up to the parent (disregarded entities)
  • Each brand is liability-walled from the others
Trade-off: more filing fees, annual reports, and bookkeeping. Worth it once a brand crosses material revenue or risk.

## Which Base Entity Do You File First?

You don't file an "S-Corp" directly. You form an LLC or Corp, then elect S-Corp tax treatment with the IRS using Form 2553.

Most common for solo founders
LLC in CT → elect S-Corp tax
  • LLC's flexibility + S-Corp's payroll-tax savings
  • Simpler annual paperwork than a full corporation
  • File IRS Form 2553 after formation
More formal
C-Corporation in CT → elect S-Corp tax
  • Requires bylaws, board minutes, shareholder records
  • Useful if you plan investors or stock options later
  • Heavier ongoing compliance

## Step-by-Step Checklist

Phase 1 · Weeks 1–2 · Foundation

  • Pick state of formation (CT)
  • Choose LLC vs. Corp with CPA
  • Reserve name with CT Secretary of State
  • File Certificate of Organization / Incorporation
  • Pay state filing fee

Phase 2 · Weeks 2–3 · Tax & Identity

  • Apply for EIN at irs.gov (free)
  • File IRS Form 2553 for S-Corp election
  • Register CT business taxes (CT-REG-1)
  • Register DBAs for each brand

Phase 3 · Weeks 3–4 · Banking & Books

  • Open business bank account (EIN required)
  • Open business credit card
  • Set up QuickBooks / Xero
  • Set up payroll (Gusto / ADP / Justworks)
  • Separate business & personal finances

Phase 4 · Weeks 4–8 · Compliance & IP

  • File USPTO trademarks (MJUEM, RCTF, Agent 508, ThinkBox)
  • Operating Agreement or Bylaws
  • Assign all existing IP to MJUEM Co. in writing
  • Update Terms / Privacy / TCPA / IP Notice docs
  • Get GL + E&O + Cyber insurance

Phase 5 · Every Year · Ongoing

  • CT Annual Report
  • Form 1120-S federal return
  • K-1 to Mona
  • CT state filings
  • Reasonable W-2 salary + distributions
  • Renew DBAs

## Risks to Avoid

!
Late Form 2553. Miss the window and you pay self-employment tax on everything for the year.
!
Zero-salary S-Corp. Taking all profit as distributions is an IRS audit trigger. Pay yourself a reasonable W-2 salary.
!
Mixing personal and business funds. Destroys your liability shield instantly. Use the business account only.
!
Undocumented IP transfer. Without a written assignment, IP still legally belongs to you personally — not MJUEM Co.
!
Expired DBAs. CT requires renewals. Lapsed names weaken brand protection.
!
Doing client work before formation. Any work done before the entity exists is personal-liability work.
